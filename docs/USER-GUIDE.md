# The songbird guide

This guide picks up the moment songbird is running and you've signed in. (If you haven't gotten it
running yet, the [README's "Get it running"](../README.md#get-it-running) walks you through that
first — it takes about fifteen minutes and no coding.) From here on, we'll move through songbird the
way you'll actually use it: reading, writing notes, and finding them again.

   up where you left off
2. [Reading](#reading) — moving around the Bible, switching translations, headings and notes, light

## Getting started in the app

This is what you see every time you open songbird:

![The songbird home page, with a verse-of-the-day card, a "pick up where you left off" link, a tally of your notes, and a list of recent notes](screenshots/welcome.png)

It greets you by name and gives you a few ways back into Scripture.

**Your account is yours.** The first person to sign up on a fresh songbird is the owner. If you share
the computer, anyone else can make their own account too — and **each person's notes are completely
private to them.** Nobody sees yours; you don't see theirs. Signing in is just the username and
password you picked.

**The verse of the day** sits at the top in your reading translation — a passage to greet you. Click
**Open** to read it in full, or **Show another** if you'd like a different one.

**Pick up where you left off.** Once you've done a little reading, a *"Continue in …"* link appears
(here it's *Continue in Genesis 2*) and drops you back exactly where you were — same book, same
chapter, same translation.

Below that, a quiet tally of how many **notes**, **sermon notes**, and **tags** you've collected, and
a list of your **recent notes** — each one a link straight back to the verse it sits on. The more you
read and write, the more these fill in. This home page becomes *yours*.

<br>

## Reading

Open a book and you're reading. Here's John chapter 3:

![The reader showing John 3 with the "Jesus and Nicodemus" heading, and a note open in a panel on the right](screenshots/reader.png)

That's the whole idea of songbird in one picture — Scripture on the left, and a note you wrote
tucked into the margin on the right.

**Getting around.** Up top, the **Book** and **Chapter** dropdowns take you anywhere; **← Prev** and
**Next →** walk you one chapter at a time. In a hurry? The **Jump to…** box understands plain
references — type something like `John 3` or `Gen 1:1` and press **Go**.

**Switching translations.** A *translation* is one English wording of the Bible — songbird offers
several (WEB, KJV, and more). Pick a different one from the **Translation** dropdown and the text
re-renders in that wording. Here's the thing that makes songbird songbird: **your notes stay anchored
to the verse, not the wording.** A note you wrote on John 3:16 shows up on John 3:16 in *every*
translation. And if a note you wrote while reading one translation turns up while you're reading
another, songbird marks it gently, so you always know where it came from.

**Section headings.** Some translations include the little editor's headings that group a passage —
like *Jesus and Nicodemus* over John 3 in the picture above. When a translation has them, they appear
right in the text, no setting to flip.

**Those small numbers in the verse.** A few Scripture engines also carry the translators' own
footnotes. If you ever see **small raised numbers** sprinkled through the text, like this —

![John 3 with small superscript numbers throughout the verses, the translators' study-note markers](screenshots/translator-notes.png)

— those are translator's notes, and tapping one opens the translators' comment on that word or
phrase. **The standard songbird setup includes none of these,** so most readers won't see them at all
— there's nothing missing if your text is clean.

**Light or dark.** The little sun/moon toggle in the top corner switches between a light theme and a
dark one — easy on the eyes in a dim room — and songbird remembers which you prefer:

![The reader in dark theme, showing John 3](screenshots/reader-dark.png)

That's reading. Everything else in songbird hangs off of it.

## Annotating

Reading is half of songbird. The other half is writing in the margins. **Click any verse number and
a notepad opens beside it** — write, and it's saved the moment you do:

![A note open beside John 1:1, with bold and italic text and a bulleted list, tagged "gospel" and "incarnation"](screenshots/note-editor.png)

**Writing a note.** The notepad takes *rich text* — formatting like you'd use in a document. The
**B**, **I**, and **Link** controls give you **bold**, *italics*, and links, and you can build
bulleted lists, just like the note on John 1:1 above. Your notes are yours and private.

**Tags.** A *tag* is a short label you stick on a note — *gospel*, *incarnation*, *comfort*, whatever
helps. Add a few and later you can round up every note that shares one (that's coming up in
[Finding things](#finding-things)).

**Linking a sermon.** songbird can also pin a **sermon** — a talk you watched or preached — to the
passage it's about. A small **▶** appears in the margin of those verses; tap it and the sermon's
details open: its title, date, any tags, and a **link to watch**:

![Psalm 23 with a ▶ marker on each verse, one open to show a sermon titled "The Lord Is My Shepherd" with its date, tags, and a watch link](screenshots/sermon.png)

Because the note is anchored to the verse, that **▶** shows up no matter which translation you're
reading. And when a passage has gathered **more than one** sermon, the marker shows a count — **▶ 2**
— and tapping it opens a little chooser so you can pick which one to open:

![Romans 8:28 with a "▶ 2" marker open to a "Sermons · 2" chooser listing two sermons](screenshots/sermon-chooser.png)

That's the heart of songbird: read a verse, mark what speaks to you, and find it again later.

## Following a church's YouTube channel

Most churches put their services on YouTube. songbird can watch a channel for you, read each
sermon's own words to find the passage it's about, and write the sermon note itself — dated the day
the service went up, tagged however you like. You'll still decide anything it can't work out, and
that turns out to be a lot of them, so there's a list for those and it's one tap each.

![The Sermon sources page, showing a followed channel with 352 needing a passage, 12 placed and 7 skipped, and the list of what songbird found below it](screenshots/sermon-sources.png)

That's the **Sources** page, in the top bar. Here's how to get there.

### First, a free key from Google

YouTube lets programs like songbird look things up, but it wants to know who's asking. So you make
a **key** — a long password-like string you paste into songbird once and then forget about. It's
free, no card is needed, and the daily allowance is far more than songbird will ever use.

It takes about five minutes, and it's all clicking. Any Google account will do.

1. **Go to [console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com) and sign in.** The first
   time you visit, a welcome box asks for your country and to accept the terms — choose your
   country, tick the box, and click **Agree and continue**.

2. **Make a project.** A *project* is just a folder Google keeps your key in. In the bar across the
   top, just to the right of the "Google Cloud" logo, there's a dropdown that says **Select a
   project** — click it, then **New project** at the top right of the box that opens. Give it any
   name you like ("songbird" is fine), leave **Location** as *No organization*, and click
   **Create**. When it's made, open that same dropdown again and pick your new project.

3. **Turn on the YouTube part.** In the search bar at the very top, type **YouTube Data API v3**.
   Click the result with exactly that name, then click the blue **Enable** button.

4. **Go to Credentials.** In the menu down the left of that page, click **Credentials**. (If you've
   clicked away and lost it, the **☰** menu at the top left has **APIs & Services → Credentials**.)

5. **Create the key.** Click **+ Create credentials** at the top, then **API key**. A box appears
   asking for a name — any name. Under **Select API restrictions**, choose **YouTube Data API v3**,
   so that *Selected APIs* lists it. Leave **Authenticate API calls through a service account**
   unticked, and leave **Application restrictions** on **None**. Click **Create**.

6. **Copy the key.** It's a long string starting with `AIza`. If you've already closed the box,
   click the key's name in the list to see it again.

7. **Give it to songbird.** In the `.env` file at the top of your songbird folder — the same file
   you may have used to point songbird at your Concord — add a line with your key after the `=`:

   ```bash
   YOUTUBE_API_KEY=AIza...your key here...
   ```

   Then start songbird again so it picks the key up:

   ```bash
   docker compose up -d --build
   ```

Treat that key like a password: it belongs in `.env`, which stays on your machine, and nowhere
else. songbird never shows it in the browser and never writes it to its logs.

Open the **Sources** page and it's ready.

### Adding a church

Click **Add source** and paste the address of the church's YouTube page:

![The "Add a source" form, with a channel link filled in, a tags box, an "Include past livestreams" tick, and a "Shortest video to count as a sermon" field](screenshots/sermon-sources-add.png)

**The link** is the channel's YouTube address — open the church's page on YouTube and copy what's
in the address bar. A link to a *playlist* works just as well, and is better if they keep one just
for sermons.

**Tags** go on every note songbird makes from this church, so *sunday* or the church's name will
let you round them all up later.

**Include past livestreams** should stay ticked for most churches — a streamed service *is* the
sermon. Untick it if the church posts an edited version afterwards and the stream is just the raw
recording.

**Shortest video to count as a sermon** is how announcements, clips and YouTube Shorts stay out of
your notes. Leave it blank and songbird uses ten minutes, which is enough. Set a number only if
this particular church is different.

### What happens next

songbird reads the whole back catalogue straight away — every video the channel has, not just the
recent ones. On a channel with hundreds of sermons that takes a minute or two, and the page tells
you it's working.

For each sermon it looks for the passage **in the video's own words**: a "Scripture:" line in the
description, the passage in the title, or the opening line of the description. When it finds one,
you get an ordinary sermon note — the kind you'd have written yourself, showing up in the reader
and in Browse, on every translation. It's dated the day the service went up on YouTube, and if a
video names two passages you get one note for each.

songbird never guesses. Every passage it finds is checked before a note is made, so a video it
can't read confidently is left for you rather than filed wrongly.

### Working the "needs a passage" list

Which brings us to the honest part. Plenty of churches title every service by its date and never
write the passage down anywhere — "Livestream Sunday Worship Service -- Sep. 06 2026". There's
nothing for songbird to read, so those sermons wait for you in a list:

![The "What songbird found" list filtered to "Needs a passage (352)", with a sermon offering "Philippians 2:1-11" as a button, a box to type a passage, and Place and "Not a sermon" buttons](screenshots/sermon-sources-review.png)

**Tap a passage and press Place.** Where songbird spotted something passage-shaped in the
description but couldn't be sure it was *the* sermon's passage, it offers it as a button. Tap it —
or tap several — and press **Place**, and you get exactly the notes songbird would have made
itself. If nothing offered is right, type the passage in the box instead.

**Not a sermon** sets a video aside — a concert, an announcement, a test stream. It's one tap, and
one tap to bring back if you change your mind.

**Clearing a long stretch at once.** Narrow the list first, by channel, by date, or by words in the
title — **More filters** opens the date range and the search box. Once you've narrowed it to
something you'd set aside wholesale, a button offers to do all of them, telling you exactly how
many and which before it does. It never touches a sermon that already has notes.

**Wrong passage** is the undo. If songbird put a note on the wrong passage, that takes its notes
back and returns the sermon to the list to be done again. Deleting the last note from a sermon over
on the Browse page does the same thing, so a sermon can never be marked done with nothing behind
it.

### Sermon notes you already wrote

If you've been writing sermon notes by hand and linking them to YouTube, they probably carry the
date you *wrote* the note rather than the day the sermon was preached. **Re-date YouTube sermons**,
in the top bar of the Sources page, fixes that: it looks each one up and shows you every date it
would change before changing anything. Nothing is written until you press **Apply**, and pressing
it twice is safe.

### Two settings you can change

Both live in that same `.env` file, and both take effect when you restart songbird.

```bash
SERMON_CHECK_INTERVAL_HOURS=168
SERMON_MIN_MINUTES=10
```

**How often songbird looks.** 168 hours is once a week, which suits a church that posts on Sundays.
The line under the page's heading always tells you what it's set to and when the next look is due.
Set it to `0` if you'd rather songbird never went looking on its own — **Check all now** still
works, and so does **Check now** on a single church.

**The shortest video that counts as a sermon.** Ten minutes by default. This is the starting point
for every church you follow; the number you set on an individual church, back in that form, wins
for that one.

### What it can't do

- **A service titled only by its date has no passage to find.** songbird isn't watching the video
  or listening to it — it reads what the church wrote. If they didn't write the passage anywhere,
  it waits in the list for you. For some churches that's most of them, and that's the honest
  outcome rather than a wrong guess.
- **Shorts, clips and anything under the minimum are skipped on purpose**, and songbird tells you
  it skipped them and why rather than hiding them.
- **Dates are the calendar day in UTC.** A service streamed Sunday morning and posted after
  midnight is still filed under the Sunday, which is what you'd expect — but if you're a long way
  from UTC, a late-evening video can occasionally land on the next day.
- **Removing the key switches the whole feature off**, and nothing else changes. The notes it
  already made are ordinary notes and stay exactly where they are.

Once it's running, this is the part of songbird that works while you're not looking: a week's
sermons, already noted, waiting in the margin the next time you read the passage.

## Study tools

There's a quiet layer of study help tucked into every verse. **Hover your mouse over a verse and a
small row of faint icons appears just after it** — each one opens a panel about *that* verse:

![John 3 with the cross-references panel open beside verse 16, listing related passages](screenshots/cross-references.png)

You don't have to memorize the icons; each one shows its name when you point at it. Here's what they
do.

**Cross-references (⇄).** A *cross-reference* is another passage that speaks to the one you're reading
— a promise echoed, a prophecy fulfilled, the same idea somewhere else. The **⇄** panel lists them
for the verse (above, the references behind John 3:16), each with a snippet so you can see what it
says; **tap one to jump straight there.**

**Topics (※).** The **※** panel shows the themes a verse sits under — the big ideas it belongs to:

![The topics panel open beside John 3:16, listing themes like "Condescension of God," "Faith," and "Salvation"](screenshots/topics-verse.png)

**Tap a topic to follow it.** songbird opens that theme and lists every verse gathered under it, each
one jump-able, with a *← Topics* link to step back:

![Drilled into the "Condescension of God" topic, showing the verses gathered under it](screenshots/topics-drill.png)

It's a way to read *across* Scripture by idea, not just straight through.

**Original language (ℵ).** The **ℵ** panel opens the verse in the language it was first written —
Hebrew in the Old Testament, Greek in the New — word by word:

![The original-language panel for Genesis 1:1, showing Hebrew word cards laid out right to left](screenshots/word-study.png)

Each card is one original word with how it's pronounced and what it means. Hebrew reads **right to
left**, so the cards run that way too, matching the order of the Hebrew text. **This is the
*original-language* words behind the verse** — it isn't a tap-an-English-word tool; it shows you what
stands under the translation.

**Tap a word** and songbird opens its dictionary entry and a **concordance** — every other place in
Scripture that same original word appears:

![A single Hebrew word's dictionary meaning and a concordance list of every verse it appears in](screenshots/word-study-strongs.png)

(Where a verse in the concordance isn't carried by your current translation, songbird says so plainly
rather than leaving a gap.)

**Places in this chapter.** One more study button lives at the top of every chapter, next to its
title: **Places in this chapter** lists the locations named in what you're reading —

![The places panel for Genesis 2, listing Eden, Euphrates, Cush, and other locations named in the chapter](screenshots/geography-panel.png)

— a quick peek at the geography right where you're reading. Seeing those places **on a map**, and
wandering the full atlas of biblical locations, gets its own section:
[Exploring places and journeys](#exploring-places-and-journeys).

## Finding things

A note you wrote last year, a verse whose words you half-remember, every passage about a theme — songbird
is built to hand them back.

**Search Scripture two ways.** Open **Search** up top. You can search **by meaning** — ask in plain
words and songbird finds the passages that *mean* that, even when they don't contain your word:

![The Search page in "Semantic" mode, the query "anxiety" returning verses about fear and worry ranked by relevance](screenshots/search.png)

That's the **Semantic** tab ("semantic" just means *by meaning*); each result shows how closely it
matches. Or flip to the **Keyword** tab to find an **exact word or phrase** — and a keyword search can
sweep **every translation at once, or just the ones you pick**, highlighting each hit so you can see how
each wording lands:

![The Search page in "Keyword" mode, the query "shepherd" highlighted across many translations at once](screenshots/search-keyword.png)

Notice the row of checkboxes under the search box: the same search can also look through **your own
notes** and through **study notes** — the translators' footnotes from [Reading](#reading). Study notes
are **only there when your Scripture engine provides them** (the standard setup ships none), so that
option simply stays quiet until it has something to find.

**Find your own notes.** Two ways, depending on what you remember. If you recall a *word* you wrote,
search for it with **Your notes** ticked in that same checkbox row:

![A keyword search with "Your notes" included in the scope](screenshots/notes-search.png)

If you recall the *tag* instead, open **Browse notes** and **filter by tag** — tap a label and songbird
rounds up every note wearing it:

![The Browse notes page, filtered to the "grace" tag, with Export and Import links in the corner](screenshots/browse.png)

**Back up your notes.** See **Export** and **Import** in the corner of Browse notes? **Export** writes
all your notes and sermons to a single file you can keep or move; **Import** loads that file on another
computer, **skipping anything you already have** so nothing doubles up. Your notes are yours — portable,
and never locked inside one machine.

## Exploring places and journeys

The "Places in this chapter" peek from [Study tools](#study-tools) has a fuller home here. songbird
gives you two ways to wander the world the Bible happens in: **every place** named in it, and a set
of **journeys** you can trace stop by stop.

**Every place, in one list.** Open **Places** up top for the *gazetteer* — a browsable index of every
location Scripture names:

![The Places page, a searchable, filterable list of locations with their type, coordinates, and a confidence label](screenshots/places-gazetteer.png)

That's more than thirteen hundred of them. **Search by name**, or narrow the list with the **Status**
filter (how sure we are of where it sat) and the **Type** filter (river, settlement, region,
mountain…). Each row tells you what kind of place it is, its coordinates, and — honestly — how
confident that location is.

**Open a place to see it up close.** Tap any name for its own page: what it is, its modern name if it
has one, the honest confidence in its location, and **every verse that mentions it** — each one a
jump straight back into the reader:

![A single place's page — Rameses — showing its details, the verses that name it, and a "Journeys through here" section](screenshots/place-detail.png)

And notice the **"Journeys through here"** section at the bottom — Rameses, it turns out, is a stop on
*The Exodus from Egypt*. That's the second way to explore.

**Follow a journey.** Open **Journeys** for the curated set — the Exodus, Paul's missionary travels,
his voyage to Rome — each listed with its passage, rough dating, and how many stops it has:

![The Journeys page, listing five curated journeys with their passages, dating, and stop counts](screenshots/journeys-list.png)

Open one and songbird draws its **route on a map with numbered stops**, followed by a **Stops** list
in order — each stop tied to the passage it comes from, ready to jump to:

![The Exodus journey, its route drawn on a map of Sinai with numbered stops and a prominent note above the map](screenshots/journey-detail.png)

**One honest caveat, shown right above the map.** A journey like the Exodus is **one scholarly
reconstruction, not a GPS track.** The route, the Red Sea crossing point, and many wilderness stops
are genuinely uncertain — some carry low or medium confidence, and songbird says so plainly rather
than pretending otherwise. Competing routes and fine-grained dating aren't drawn at all. And a stop
whose location nobody knows is still **listed in order — just marked "Location unknown" and left off
the map** rather than pinned to a guess. You're seeing a careful best-reading of the text, with its
seams left honest.

## Comparing translations

Sometimes the clearest way to understand a verse is to set wordings side by side. Open **Compare**:

![The Compare page showing John 3 in three translations — NET, AKJV, and ASV — in parallel columns, lined up verse for verse](screenshots/compare.png)

You can read **up to three translations at once**, each in its own column, **lined up verse for
verse** — so verse 3 in one column sits right beside verse 3 in the others. Pick the translation at
the top of each column; the Book, Chapter, and **← Prev / Next →** controls work just like the
reader. Any **notes you've written show up in the column they belong to** (here they're read-only —
Compare is for reading across, not editing).

It's the quickest cure for a puzzling phrase: a line that reads stiffly in one translation often comes
clear the moment you see how another renders it.

## Your data

That's the whole of songbird. A few things worth keeping in mind as you go.

**Your notes are yours.** Each account's notes are private to the person who wrote them — nobody else
who uses this songbird can see yours (see [Getting started](#getting-started-in-the-app)).

**They're portable.** You're never locked in: the **Export** and **Import** buttons in Browse notes
save all your notes and sermons to a single file and load them on another computer — the walkthrough
is back in [Finding things](#finding-things).

**Read however suits the room.** The light/dark toggle is always in the top corner, and songbird
remembers your choice (more in [Reading](#reading)).

That's everything. Open a chapter, click a verse, and write the first thing that speaks to you — the
rest of songbird is just here to help you find it again. 🕊️
